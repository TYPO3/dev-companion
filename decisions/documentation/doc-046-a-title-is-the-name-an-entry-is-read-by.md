# D-DOC-046 — A title is the name an entry is read by

**A title says one thing in twelve words or so, and what it is not is the
statement said again.**

The statement has a thirty-word bound and fails a check. The title had no
measure at all, so it grew into the sentence below it.

## Evidence

- Measured on 2026-08-23 over 446 decisions. The mean title is 65 characters and
  twelve words, the longest 116 characters, and 227 run past twelve words or
  join two claims.
- A title reuses 46% of its own words from its own statement, and 68 of the 446
  reuse three quarters or more. That is the mechanism: with nothing to be
  instead, the title became the statement.
- 89 join two claims outright, with a comma-and, a dash or a semicolon. "The
  Extbase fork is delivered by the content-element intent, and it forks on the
  request rather than on the category". Every one of the first twelve read had
  its second clause in the statement already, near-verbatim.
- The requirement corpus is the control, and it is healthy: 222 titles, mean
  nine words, fourteen past twelve, two that join two claims. A requirement's
  title names a demand and its statement makes it, which is the split this
  restores for a decision.

## Decided

- Twelve words as the measure, and `bin/cli prose:check` reports it beside the
  sentence count. Nothing fails: 227 entries broke it on its first day, and a
  rule that many entries break is a rewrite in a rule's clothes. That is
  `D-DOC-002`'s account of the same problem one corpus over.
- 69 titles got shorter here: the ones that both ran long and joined two claims.
  There the cut is unambiguous because the dropped clause is in the statement.
  The 23 that join two claims and stay short stay as they are. "Every stage is a
  directory, and closing is none" is one thought with its consequence. A cut
  would lose the point rather than a repetition.
- The 153 that run long and join nothing are the report's to carry. A cut to one
  is a judgement about which words earn their place. A sweep would take the half
  that tells them apart off the family of entries titled "… is a gap this server
  owns".
- File names stay as they are. A slug is a name rather than a claim about today.
  A rename of 69 files would rewrite every link that names one for nothing a
  reader gains.

## Assumed

- That the second clause of a joined title is always in the statement. It was in
  the twelve read side by side, and the rest got shorter on the same basis with
  no word-for-word check of each statement.
- That twelve is the number. It is where the requirement corpus already sits
  rather than a measure of what reads well. What would settle it is a listing
  nobody can scan at that length.

## Wrong if

- The count sits where it is for a month while sessions write and read entries.
  Then a report is not enough for a title either. The measure needs a place in
  the work rather than a line in a check. That is the same failure `D-DOC-002`
  names for the sentence count.
- A cut title turns out to have carried the half a listing exists for, and
  somebody has to open the entry to tell two apart. `D-ANS-045` shrank into
  `R-ANS-020`'s title on the first pass and got another, so the failure has
  already happened once.
- New titles follow the measure and no longer say what the entry settled. Twelve
  words is where a name fits, not a budget to spend.

## Since then

123 more titles got shorter once the file name became the title's, and what the
measure counts fell from 227 to 55. Those are the 23 that join two claims and
stay short, and about 19 a word or two long. That is where it stopped, and the
shape is the reason. The affirmative "A is B, not C" is what the format asks
for. A cut of the second half takes the half that tells an entry from its
neighbours. The file names moved with them, which is the pressure `D-DOC-047`
assumed.

The family left to the report got new titles on the other half of the rule
(`D-DOC-056`): 38 titles got longer. The trade this entry did not price is that
the half that tells them apart stayed verbatim. So what the extra words buy is a
listing that reads as one kind of entry.
