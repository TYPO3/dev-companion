# D-DOC-055 — A revoked entry is one whose Wrong if fired

**An entry whose queued work landed gains a **Since then** and keeps the status
its visit left. `revoked` stays what a **Wrong if** that fired leaves.**

Nine entries were in one situation, the gap they named has its fill, and they
closed out two ways. So a listing showed four carried-out decisions among the
dead ends.

## Evidence

- The rule is already written.
  [`decisions.rst`](../../documentation/records/decisions.rst) says the status
  names the last dated section. A visit that settles the **Wrong if** changes
  it, and a visit that settles neither leaves it and adds a **Since then**.
- Four entries carried a `Revoked on` under a `Confirmed on` whose **Wrong if**
  had its visit and had not fired. `D-KNW-072`, `D-KNW-074`, `D-KNW-103` and
  `D-ANS-029`. Three of them open that section with the same sentence — "By the
  work this entry queued".
- Five entries in the same situation stayed confirmed: `D-KNW-027`, `D-KNW-065`,
  `D-KNW-076`, `D-KNW-099` and `D-KNW-100`. `D-KNW-076`'s visit says it
  outright: "Neither **Wrong if** fired, and that is the finding".
- Both practices ran on the same two days, 2026-08-14 and 2026-08-18, so this
  was two answers to one question rather than a practice that moved.
- `D-KNW-040` is the revocation the rule keeps. Its first **Wrong if** is what
  happened, read on 2026-08-03, and the entry says which.
- What it cost is where the listing puts them.
  [`writing-a-decision.rst`](../../documentation/records/writing-a-decision.rst)
  keeps the revoked entries in a run of their own "because mixed into the rest
  they looked exactly like something to build on". So the four read as dead ends
  and the visit each carries stood as withdrawn.

## Decided

- The four move to `confirmed` and their `revokedBy` goes. What the visit found
  stands; what the work did is not a reason to withdraw it.
- Where the `Confirmed on` already named the successor, the `Revoked on` section
  goes. `D-KNW-072` names `D-KNW-073` in the visit itself, so a conversion would
  say one thing twice. Where only the revocation carried the pointer, it becomes
  a **Since then** and keeps it.
- A **Since then** carries no date, so `Decisions::datedLines()` does not read
  it and the status still names the `Confirmed on` above it. No check changes.
- **Where a revoked entry has a part that still holds, a decision that covers
  that part is what carries it, and `revokedBy` names it.** `D-DIS-002` left the
  DDEV half to the queue and `D-DIS-007` took it; `D-KNW-006` answered
  `D-KNW-001`'s domain half. Both were that pattern with the link absent, and
  both now name their successor. This is the second half of what
  [`D-DOC-003`](doc-003-a-decision-says-what-came-back.md) settled. It weighed
  an `amended` state for the partly-wrong entries and dropped it. A state that
  says "some of this is safe" does not say which. What it left the reader is the
  revocation's own dated line, and what this adds is the entry that carries the
  half that still holds.
- Not a check. Whether a **Wrong if** fired is a judgement, and nothing here can
  hold a dated section's prose to one. What keeps this is the session that
  writes the section and asks which of the two it is.
- The concern the four raised is real and the title is where its answer lies. A
  confirmed entry that says "X is missing from the corpus" reads as a live gap.
  That is why the family it belongs to got new titles, `D-DOC-056`.

## Assumed

- That the four readings were as complete as their `Confirmed on` says. Each was
  reread here for whether a **Wrong if** fired, not repeated against the
  checkouts.
- That no entry needs a third state for "carried out". Nine were enough to see
  the split and none of them wanted a word `confirmed` does not have.

## Wrong if

- A reader takes a confirmed entry whose statement says "X is missing from the
  corpus" for a live gap and writes X a second time. The state would then carry
  a claim the title should carry, and the title sweep would not have been
  enough.
- A revoked entry turns up whose `Revoked on` names a part that still holds and
  no decision carries it. Four do today, `D-CAT-002`, `D-KNW-002`, `D-SCO-001`
  and `D-SCO-004`, and `todo/open/2026-08-23-120000` is what reads them. If that
  visit finds nobody owes a decision after all, this bullet is where the rule is
  too strict.
- A session writes a `Revoked on` for work it carried out anyway. The rule would
  then be one nobody applies at the moment of need, and a report over the dated
  sections is the lever that remains.

## Since then

The four revocations had their read against the repository and two wanted a
successor; both now name one. A requirement carries the other two, so the rule
is one word too narrow. What carries a part that survives is an entry of either
kind. `revokedBy` names it only where that entry is a decision, because the
field takes one decision id. A requirement held by a test that reads the
sentence the revocation kept is the stronger carrier. One entry belongs on
neither list: its revocations replace the mechanism whole.
