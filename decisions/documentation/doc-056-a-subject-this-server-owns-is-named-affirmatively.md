# D-DOC-056 — A subject this server owns is named affirmatively

**The 38 entries that judged a subject to be inside this server's boundary carry
the title "… is a subject this server owns". They named the deficiency
instead.**

The title rule says an entry names the work and not the gap it closes, and the
largest family in the corpus was the exception.

## Evidence

- The rule is
  [`writing-a-decision.rst`](../../documentation/records/writing-a-decision.rst)'s:
  a title "says what will be done, in the affirmative. Not the deficiency it
  corrects". And "a listing where some titles name a fix and others name a gap
  is read as two kinds of entry when there is one".
- 38 titles carried "is a gap" or "is a gap this server owns", read on
  2026-08-23. It is the largest title family in the corpus.
- Every one of them decided the same thing. Each is a step 1a or 1b judgement:
  the subject is inside this server's boundary and the work sits in the queue.
  Their statements say so in one of three wordings. "inside this server's
  boundary and missing from it", "Nothing below `knowledge/` says …". And "The
  corpus states …", written of what the queued work would land.
- The affirmative form was already in the corpus, on entries of exactly this
  kind. `D-KNW-063` "What a TCA type stores is a subject this server owns". And
  `D-KNW-071` "… is a procedure this server carries" for the two that queue a
  document rather than hints.
- What the old form cost is the listing. 38 settled judgements read as open
  deficiencies. Against them the entries that wrote the corpus statement,
  `D-KNW-073`, `078`, `079`, `084`, `090`, `104`, read as the only ones that had
  done anything.

## Decided

- The tail becomes "is a subject this server owns", and "is a procedure this
  server carries" for `D-KNW-102`, whose queued work is a document.
- The subject half stays verbatim, because that is what a reader scans and what
  tells the 38 apart. `D-KNW-026` is the one exception. "Where a one-off script
  may not be written" is a negation, and it becomes "Where a one-off script
  belongs".
- The length report gets 38 entries longer and that is the price. `D-DOC-002`
  and `D-DOC-046` both leave the word count to the report, and a listing exists
  for the sameness across a family. A shorter tail without "this server" would
  have taken the claim with it.
- `bin/cli decisions:rename` did the move, 38 files, 109 references rewritten,
  so no path came from a hand.
- The statements are not touched. Several say "The corpus states …" of work in
  the queue rather than landed. That is a claim about a tense rather than about
  a title, and it is not what this sweep read for.

## Assumed

- That "subject" is the word. It is `D-KNW-063`'s and it is what
  `knowledge/server-scope.json` calls the things it covers, so it came from
  there rather than from a choice.

## Wrong if

- A session looks for an entry by the words "is a gap" and finds none. The
  family was findable under a wrong name and is now findable under the right
  one, and nothing redirects the old one.
- An entry lands whose judgement is that a subject is **outside** the boundary.
  "is a subject this server owns" would then have a counterpart nobody has
  named, and the family would be half a vocabulary.
- The tail turns out to carry no information a reader uses, so 38 listings end
  in six identical words. The lever would be a drop of it entirely with the
  subject alone, which is shorter than the rejected form here.

## Since then

The **Decided** sections had their read, which the entry had left as an
assumption, and two of the 38 were not the one kind. One names step 2 rather
than step 1a: the rule was in the corpus and the gap is a clause it does not
name. The other names no step at all and decides where a hint goes. Both got
titles in the families they belong to. The other 36 hold, four with a second
half beside step 1a and three that name the queue and number no rung.
