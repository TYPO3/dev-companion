# R-FBK-015 — A feedback field that was cut says so

**A field cut for length carries a marker where the cut fell, and the answer
names the field and how much of it went.**

The archive keeps a session's report because the report is the evidence, so a
report that differs from what the session wrote says so. That is the ground the
redaction marker stands on. What a cut needs it for is worse than what a
redaction does. A redaction leaves the name of the value beside its marker. A
cut falls on a character count, mid-word, and takes with it every sign that the
sentence went anywhere. Read months later, a report that stopped short and a
report that ended are the same file.

The channel owes both halves, because they reach different readers. The marker
is for whoever judges the feedback, who was not there. The answer is for the
session that wrote it. That session is the last thing anywhere that still holds
the part that went. It can record it again in fewer words while it does.

A field that is exactly as long as it may be gets no marker. Nothing came off
it, and a marker there says of the report something that is not true.

The subject carries the `...` its title ends in instead of a marker, because a
listing shows a title. It owes the answer half like every other field, which is
the half that reaches the session that still holds the line
([`D-FBK-049`](../../decisions/feedback/fbk-049-a-stored-field-states-the-cap-it-is-cut-at.md)).

## From

`feedback/2026-08-03-144316` (2026-08-03), whose observation is exactly 4000
characters and ends `the skill fixed the or`. A session found it while it judged
it onto `D-FBK-018`. What the cut took was the sentence that named the shape the
session reported, which is the half the judgement turned on. Neither the file
nor the answer of `typo3_feedback_record` said anything had gone. One field in
235 recorded feedback sits on the cap, so this is rare rather than widespread.
Nobody can detect it from the file, which is why it is worth a line.
