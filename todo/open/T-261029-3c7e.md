# Where a new site has a decision to make, this corpus states no options

Two runs of 2026-10-29 built a sitepackage from one design and one sentence.
The questions the design leaves open were settled by each run alone, and the
two runs did not settle them alike. This corpus covers the APIs of TYPO3 well,
and the decisions a new site makes hardly at all.

1. Say that an accessible variant is a content model decision before it is a
   language decision. Both runs ended with Easy Read as a site language, and
   Opus reached that by deleting the page it had built first. A variant that needs its own elements is not a
   translation of the elements beside it. `content-model-vs-markup` in
   `knowledge/hints/content-elements.json` is the same rule one level up. What
   Easy Read itself requires stays out: that is accessibility practice rather
   than TYPO3.
2. Say what `fallbackType` decides, and that `strict` leaves an untranslated
   record absent rather than English. The word appears nowhere below
   `knowledge/` today. A site that launches with a partial translation is the
   case that decides it.
3. Say what a sent mail carries. The template EXT:form ships for its email
   finishers declares `<f:layout name="SystemEmail" />`, so a public contact
   form renders the TYPO3 logo and `typo3.information.copyrightNotice` until a
   site replaces the layout. `EmailFinisher` takes `templateRootPaths`,
   `layoutRootPaths`, `partialRootPaths` and `templateName` for that, and
   `BE/loginLogo` changes the logo in a mail and on the backend login at once.
   State that the finisher is built to take another layout, because the
   notice's own wording reads as a bar on replacing it. The statement belongs
   beside `form-framework`, which a session that sends mail already reads.
4. Say which doktype holds a group of pages. A folder, doktype 254, or a
   spacer, doktype 199, is the container, and a page hidden from the menu stays
   a page with an address. `PagesXmlSitemapDataProvider` excludes `3, 4, 6, 7,
   199, 254` and reads `nav_hide` nowhere, so the doktype decides whether the
   XML sitemap lists the container. The statement belongs beside the
   `page-rendering` statement on what the menu processor excludes.
5. Say what `indexed_search` indexes without a crawler, and name the
   alternatives. `typo3/cms-indexed-search` is released for 14.3 and is not
   part of `typo3/cms-base-distribution`.
6. Say what deletes form data and what does not. `form:cleanup:uploads` and
   `DeleteUploadsFinisher` cover the uploaded files alone, and nothing in
   `typo3/cms-form` deletes a row that `SaveToDatabase` wrote. The statement
   belongs beside `form-framework` in `knowledge/hints/form.json`. How long a
   site keeps personal data is law rather than TYPO3.
7. Weigh the core way of building an element against content blocks. That one
   needs a decision before a hint, because it names a package outside the core.
   `knw-012` records what happened when a session blurred that line. Whatever
   the decision settles, such a statement says that the package is not core.
8. Route the new statements from the intents a greenfield session reaches, and
   hold each route with a test. `T-261029-9f41` carries the route for the
   statements that already exist; this card carries the statements that do not.

Verify every fact against `.checkouts/` before it goes into `knowledge/`. The
machine the runs stood on has no checkout, so the observations in the feedback
are evidence of the gap rather than evidence of the fact.

The judging step comes first, as `documentation/records/judging.rst` says. The
feedback stays open until the commit that ships the last statement archives it.
